## Problem map

`method-lab/f-map@1.0.0` · material

Make boundaries, stakeholders, constraints and success criteria explicit.

### Problem

An unclear problem scope makes relevant information difficult to identify.

### When to use

Entering an unfamiliar or poorly defined problem

### Prerequisites

- A problem and an observable desired result can be described.

### Limitations

- The source gives no universal success guarantee or quantitative threshold.
- Order and suitability depend on the problem and available evidence.

### Rationale

An explicit boundary helps decide which information is relevant.

### Success checks

- Boundary, stakeholders, constraints and observable success criterion are explicit.

### Steps

1. Record the problem boundary and stakeholders.
   - Input: The problem to be addressed.
   - Output: An explicit scope and stakeholder list.
   - Check: Check whether each listed item belongs to the scope.
2. Record constraints and an observable success criterion.
   - Input: Known constraints and desired result.
   - Output: Constraints and a success criterion.
   - Check: Check that the criterion can be observed.

## Evidence chain

`method-lab/f-evidence@1.0.0` · material

Connect conclusions to observations and competing explanations.

### Problem

A claim has weak support or several possible explanations.

### When to use

Judging whether a claim is well supported

### Prerequisites

- A claim and relevant observations are available for examination.

### Limitations

- The source gives no universal success guarantee or quantitative threshold.
- Order and suitability depend on the problem and available evidence.

### Rationale

A single observation should not be treated as proof.

### Success checks

- Observations and interpretations are separate; competing explanations were considered.

### Steps

1. Separate observations from explanations.
   - Input: The claim and available observations.
   - Output: A distinction between observed facts and interpretations.
   - Check: Check that interpretations are not presented as direct observations.
2. Seek competing explanations and counterevidence.
   - Input: The current explanation.
   - Output: Alternative explanations and relevant evidence.
   - Check: Do not treat one observation as proof; the source gives no numeric evidence threshold.

## Action loop

`method-lab/f-action@1.0.0` · material

Test an assumption, compare prediction and outcome, then revise.

### Problem

Discussion stalls without information to guide a decision.

### When to use

Turning understanding into a small informative action

### Prerequisites

- An important assumption can be tested at small scale.

### Limitations

- The source gives no universal success guarantee or quantitative threshold.
- Order and suitability depend on the problem and available evidence.

### Rationale

A test can produce new information and change the next decision.

### Success checks

- A test result was compared with a prediction before scaling.

### Steps

1. Choose a small, low-cost test of an important assumption.
   - Input: An assumption and prediction.
   - Output: An informative test and its result.
   - Check: Check whether the test produces new information.
2. Compare the result with the prediction and revise before scaling.
   - Input: Predicted and actual results.
   - Output: A revised assumption or next decision.
   - Check: Use changed constraints to revisit the problem map where needed.
